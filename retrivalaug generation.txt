Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is an AI framework that improves large language model (LLM) responses by grounding them in external, verifiable data. Instead of relying solely on static training data, the model first retrieves relevant facts from a custom knowledge base and then uses that specific context to generate a highly accurate, up-to-date response.Why Use Retrieval-Augmented Generation?Reduces Hallucinations: Prevents the AI from making up facts by forcing it to answer based on trusted, provided documents.Up-to-Date Knowledge: Pulls the latest, real-time information without requiring expensive model retraining.Domain Expertise: Tailors responses to internal corporate data, specialized medical literature, or legal documents.How RAG WorksRetrieval Phase: When you ask a question, the system converts your query into mathematical representations (embeddings) and searches a vector database for relevant document chunks.Augmentation: The retrieved information is appended directly to your original prompt.Generation: The language model reads the augmented prompt and synthesizes a natural, highly informed response.For a beginner-friendly, visual breakdown of how embeddings and document stores fit into the RAG workflow:1mHow to use Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)Google Cloud TechYouTube · 24 Oct 2024RAG ComponentsDocument Store: The repository holding your custom information (e.g., PDFs, company wikis, or enterprise databases).Vector Database: A specialized database that indexes your documents using mathematical representations, allowing for rapid similarity searches.Generator: The LLM that reads your query and the retrieved context to draft the final response.Would you like to explore how to implement a RAG pipeline, or are you looking to understand the different types of RAG architectures like GraphRAG and Agentic RAG?
